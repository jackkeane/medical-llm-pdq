Chapter 3

Distillation: the model it used to be teaches the model it became

Pruning left the stage-3 model 13 points worse. Knowledge distillation repairs it by putting the original, uncompressed checkpoint beside the pruned one: on every training example the pruned student is trained to match the teacher's full output distribution, not just the right answer. Eighty steps per stage later, the most damaged model had climbed from 62 to 80/100.

Why copy a teacher instead of just re-training?

A training label carries one fact: "the answer is yes." The teacher's output carries much more: how confident yes should be, whether maybe was a close second, which phrasings are plausible next tokens. Matching that whole probability distribution — the field calls it dark knowledge — gives the student a far denser training signal per example, which is exactly what you want when repairing damage with only 800 examples and 80 optimizer steps. A temperature of T = 2 softens both distributions (divides the logits before softmax) so the runner-up probabilities become visible enough to learn from.

The loss is a weighted blend, straight from run_iterative_distillation.py:

loss = 0.6 * CE(student, labels)              # stay correct on the data
     + 0.4 * KL(teacher/T ‖ student/T) * T²  # match the teacher's distribution, T = 2

# per stage: 80 steps · AdamW, lr 1e-4 · batch 1 · 512 tokens
# same 800 medical training samples the original finetune used
The detail that makes this work: the zeros must survive training. Naively finetuning a pruned model un-prunes it — gradient updates write new values over the zeros. Here the student's pruned weights are frozen (loaded in 4-bit NF4, exactly like QLoRA in the sibling walkthrough) and all learning flows through a small fresh LoRA adapter (r=16) attached only to the attention projections — layers that were never pruned. The MLPs receive no updates, so training cannot un-prune them — the 2:4 sparsity survives intact. Bonus: the trainable set is tiny, so 80 repair steps are cheap. And with the fp32 teacher running on the CPU while the 4-bit student trains on the GPU, the whole two-model setup fits one consumer machine.

The results: the most damaged student gained the most

Each pruning stage got its own distillation pass. "Before" is the pruned student re-scored in its 4-bit training setup — which is why stage 1 reads 72 here, not Chapter 2's 77: quantized evaluation wobbles the number a little (Chapter 5 tables every variant).

The pattern is the textbook one: the deeper the pruning damage, the more the teacher has to offer. Stage 3 — the model that lost the most — recovered +18 points and ended at 80/100, six above the original dense baseline of 74. That made it the obvious candidate to carry into quantization: maximum sparsity and top score.

Can a pruned model really beat its own baseline? Be skeptical with the run, not for it. Three honest ingredients: ±4 points of exam noise (Chapter 1), distillation's regularizing effect on a small dataset, and the fact that the student got 80 extra steps of medical training the baseline never had. The defensible claim is full recovery at 39% sparsity — which is remarkable enough — not "pruning made it smarter".
